v1.0.3
- The queue is ranked by track miles instead of ground distance. Ground
  distance alone treated an aircraft seven miles out at 11,000 ft as though it
  were about to touch down, ahead of one on final at 2,000 ft. Height an
  aircraft still has to lose now counts as distance it still has to fly.
- The same measure decides what reaches the board at all, so something close to
  the field but far too high to be landing is left off rather than announced as
  landing next.
- Departures are judged on a climb gradient rather than an approach one.
- The board shows the IATA flight number where it is known, MH784 rather than
  the radio callsign MAS784, matching what airport boards and tracking apps
  display so a flight can actually be looked up.

v1.0.2
- The board is the runway queue. Instead of one arrival and one departure, it
  shows the two or three aircraft closest to the runway, whichever they are, so
  during an arrival rush you see the whole landing sequence.
- Only two words now: LANDING and TAKE OFF. ON FINAL, APPROACHING, CLIMBING OUT
  and INBOUND read as jargon and made the board harder to take in at a glance.
- Aircraft further out than the approach range are left off entirely rather
  than shown under softer wording, so everything on the board is really
  landing or leaving. Defaults to 15 nm.
- Two new settings: how many aircraft the board shows, and how close they must
  be to reach it.

v1.0.1
- What an aircraft is plainly doing now outranks the route database. Long
  flights that keep one callsign across several sectors, such as KL843 from
  Amsterdam through Bangkok to Taipei, were showing as departures while
  visibly landing, because the database only holds the onward leg.
- Where that happens the board says CONTINUES TO <destination> rather than
  claiming the flight started at the airport it is landing at.
- A climb near the runway no longer overrides a known arrival, so a go-around
  stays an arrival instead of flipping to a departure.

v1.0.0
- Split-flap departure board showing what is happening right now: the aircraft
  on final approach and the one climbing out, rather than a list to scan.
- Airline logos, fetched once per airline and cached, with a text badge when no
  trustworthy logo is available.
- Three views: board only, board with radar, board with map.
- Bundled vector map of the upper Gulf coastline, the Chao Phraya, and the
  runway layouts at Suvarnabhumi and Don Mueang. No network needed.
- Radar and map rotate to match your window, with the visible slice of sky
  marked; the aircraft on the board are picked out bright and labelled.
- Arrivals, departures and overflights are independent switches.
- Non-airline traffic is kept off the board using the ADS-B emitter category.
- Feeds: adsb.lol, adsb.fi, or a local dump1090/tar1090 receiver.
- Route and airframe lookups via adsbdb.com, capped per refresh and cached on
  disk so an evening at the window is almost entirely cache hits.
- Optional background service that notifies when a low aircraft passes close.
